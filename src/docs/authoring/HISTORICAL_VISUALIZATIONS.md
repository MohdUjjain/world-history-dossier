# Historical visualization policy — World History Dossier

**Status: Phase C editorial assessment (November 2026).** This note records a deliberate selection standard, not a claim that any new maps, historical photographs, or verified graphics have been added. No chapter text or history claims were changed in Phase C.

## What already earns space in the dossier

The authored chapters already use structured historical **timelines**, **comparison tables**, **profiles**, **flow sequences**, and **study-aid diagrams**. Their content is selectable text and their topic/section pairing is retained in the PDF and flowing screen reader. Before introducing a decorative image, prefer improving an existing source-grounded representation.

## When a new graphic is justified

A historical image or map should answer a concrete question that prose cannot answer as clearly, such as **where** a documented territorial shift occurred, **when** several sourced events overlapped, or **how** a documented diplomatic chain is structured. Evaluate each proposal against:

1. **Source traceability:** the chapter text or an explicitly identified supplementary source supports *every* label, arrow, boundary and date. Do not infer omitted actors or directions.
2. **Chronological precision:** boundary maps and alliances must declare the represented year/date, not present a changing political map as timeless.
3. **Rights and scholarly credit:** use public-domain/licensed material or newly authored vector artwork; include credit/license in the companion editorial notes where applicable.
4. **Accessibility:** give each figure a descriptive caption, plain-language explanation, and alternative text or equivalent data table.
5. **Screen and print parity:** design a readable screen figure with a deliberate 16:9 print placement. Verify text does not become tiny and the source is not split or clipped during PDF pagination.
6. **Selectivity:** no automatic stock images or generic globe decorations. Limit new graphics to chapters with a demonstrable educational payoff.

## Recommended order

- **First:** preserve and polish the chapter-native timelines, causal comparisons and educational cards already in the source (no outside historical claims).
- **Second:** propose one *dated, source-supported* thematic map when the authoring material explicitly provides the geography and chronology. Review attribution/rights and its mobile and printed legibility before implementation.
- **Third:** consider a small set of sourced archival photographs only if they add historical evidence beyond illustration, with caption and origin notes.

**Decision for Phase C:** no new unsourced historical maps or pictures. The complete-reader usability refinements and direct bookmarked-PDF access take priority. An illustration can be a future chapter-by-chapter editorial task, not a bulk stylistic insert.
